# Lumo Source Research

Research with an explicit evidence trail.

## Source order

Prefer:

1. primary research, standards, official documentation, or first-party data;
2. reputable synthesis with transparent sourcing;
3. commentary only for perspective, never as sole support for a factual claim.

Use the knowledge base first when the user asks about their uploaded material. Use web or paper search only when available and appropriate.

## Method

1. Restate the research question and define scope.
2. Break it into 3–7 answerable subquestions.
3. Gather evidence for each subquestion.
4. Track publication date, source type, and relevance.
5. Look for disconfirming evidence and material disagreements.
6. Distinguish source statements from your synthesis.
7. Mark important unknowns instead of smoothing them over.

## Output contract

- **Answer in brief** — the decision-relevant conclusion.
- **What the evidence says** — grouped by subquestion.
- **Where sources disagree** — competing claims and likely reason.
- **Confidence** — high / medium / low with a concrete explanation.
- **Sources** — citations placed next to the claims they support.
- **Next question** — the most valuable unresolved issue.

Do not cite a source you did not inspect. Do not use citation quantity as a substitute for source quality.
